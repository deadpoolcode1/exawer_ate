======================================================================
MAC aging finishes LATER than the aging time, and the test asked once
======================================================================
pc-3099 (10.3.99.1), 8.7.0 LAB 935, 2026-09-09.

SYMPTOM
  TC03 ran 128 passing assertions and failed 4, all of them the aging
  ones:

    Fail: The output of show evpn mac-address-table name evi-1 source
          x-eth0/0/40.1003 STILL contains lines that should have gone:
          [00:00:02:00:00:01 ... x-eth0/0/40.1003 ...]
    Fail: The output of show bgp l2vpn evpn table evi detail STILL
          contains lines that should have gone:
          [Type=2: VLAN-ID=0, MAC=00:00:02:00:00:01]

  The obvious reading is that aging is broken. It is not.

WHAT THE DEVICE ACTUALLY DID
  Read off the same run, plus a direct check afterwards:

    15:44:52   AC3's last frame (traffic item suspended)
    15:50:00   entry STILL present   (308 s after)  -> Fail
    15:50:34   entry STILL present   (342 s after)  -> Fail
    15:52:14   entry GONE            (442 s after)

    show evpn detail -> MAC Aging Time: 300 seconds

  So the entry outlived its nominal aging time by at least 42 s and was
  gone within 442 s. The device ages on a 300 s timer but SWEEPS on a
  coarser one. Asking once at exactly 300 s is a race the test loses,
  and it loses it against a device that is behaving correctly.

  AC1's MAC was still present throughout, correctly: AC1 never stops
  transmitting, so its entry is refreshed. That is the control which
  shows the table itself had not simply emptied.

THE DEFECT WAS OURS, AND IT WAS NOT THE WAIT
  `verifyShowLinesAbsent` read the command output EXACTLY ONCE and
  asserted on it. Every other verification in the suite polls; this one
  did not, and it is the one whose whole subject is a thing that takes
  time to stop being true.

  Fixed by polling until the lines are gone or a budget expires, with
  the budget declared per step:

    AGING_VERIFY_TIMEOUT_IN_MSEC = 240000

  240 s past the nominal wait covers the 442 s that was measured, with
  margin. Lengthening the WAIT instead would have hidden the real
  shape of the problem; polling states it: the test fails if the rows
  never go, not if they have not gone yet.

WHY THIS IS NOT A WEAKENED ASSERTION
  The assertion still fails if aging does not happen. It cannot pass on
  a device that never removes the entry, because the loop ends at the
  deadline and asserts what it last saw. What changed is that the test
  no longer demands the device be finished at a moment the device never
  promised.
